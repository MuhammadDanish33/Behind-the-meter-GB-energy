# Behind the Meter: GB Embedded Renewable Impact, Duck Curve Dynamics & System Flexibility Pressures

**Author:** Muhammad Danish  
**Dataset:** NESO Half-Hourly Electricity Outturn Data  
**Tool:** Microsoft Power BI  

---

## Summary

This Power BI project analyses how embedded renewable generation — solar panels and wind turbines connected to the local distribution network — suppresses GB's transmission-recorded electricity demand, reshapes the daily demand profile (the "duck curve"), and creates ramp-rate pressure for system operators.

Built across five interactive dashboard pages using only NESO's half-hourly operational dataset, the analysis shows that **4,080 MW of generation is invisible to transmission metering on average every half-hour** — and that the steepest single demand surge in the dataset reached **3,693 MW in just 30 minutes**.

---

## Business Problem

The National Demand (ND) figure published by NESO does not capture all the electricity that GB consumers use. When solar panels and wind turbines generate power at distribution level, they supply local consumers directly — before that consumption ever reaches a transmission meter. The result is that the official ND figure systematically understates true underlying demand.

As embedded solar and wind capacity grows, this invisible gap widens. More importantly, the *shape* of the daily demand curve is changing: solar generation creates a midday dip in ND, which then surges back steeply in the evening as solar output falls to zero. This "duck curve" shape has direct consequences for how flexibly the power system must be operated — and for when and where flexibility is most urgently needed.

---

## Business Impact

The dashboard quantifies this problem with real operational data:

- **4,080 MW** of electricity is generated by embedded wind and solar on average every half-hour but never shows up in the transmission demand figure — it is consumed locally before reaching the grid
- This represents a **13.50% suppression ratio**: roughly 1 in every 8 megawatts of electricity consumed is served by generation that official demand figures do not record
- The true average consumption of GB consumers — once embedded generation is added back — is **30,240 MW**, significantly above what transmission metering alone would suggest
- Peak transmission-recorded demand reaches **46,000 MW**
- The steepest demand surge recorded is **3,693 MW in a single 30-minute period** — the speed at which generation must ramp up to follow evening demand recovery
- Across the standard evening peak window, the average ramp rate is **776.71 MW per half-hour** — a recurring, predictable pressure that repeats every day

For any organisation operating in, investing in, or advising on the GB energy system, these figures define the size of the problem the grid must manage.

## Objectives

- Quantify how much embedded wind and solar generation suppresses transmission-metered demand, and identify when this effect is largest
- Reconstruct GB's gross underlying demand (true consumption) and compare it to the metered ND profile by time of day and season
- Measure the capacity factor performance of embedded wind and solar assets across every half-hour of the day and every month of the year
- Identify the steepest intra-day demand ramp events, rank them, and show when they occur
- Present how net interconnector flows and pump storage activity relate to ramp-stress periods — as observational context only, without causal claims

## Key Findings

*Every finding below is directly supported by the Power BI KPI cards and dashboard visuals. No numbers are estimated or assumed.*

### 1 — The Suppression Gap Is 13.50% of True Demand
On average, **4,080 MW** of electricity consumed every half-hour never appears in GB's National Demand figure. It is served by embedded wind and solar generation that offsets local demand before the meter. At a suppression ratio of **13.50%**, any analyst or forecaster working from ND alone is working with a figure that understates true consumption by that margin.

### 2 — True Underlying Demand Is 30,240 MW on Average
Once embedded generation is added back, the true average underlying demand is **30,240 MW** — the correct baseline for any analysis of how much energy GB consumers actually use.

### 3 — The Steepest Demand Ramp Is 3,693 MW in 30 Minutes
The largest single intra-day demand ramp recorded in the dataset is **3,693 MW in one half-hour period**. Across the standard evening peak window, the average ramp rate is **776.71 MW per half-hour**. This is the recurring operational pressure that flexibility providers, storage assets, and interconnectors must absorb every evening.

### 4 — Winter and Autumn Dominate the Top 20 Ramp Events
The Top 20 ramp events table on the dashboard shows that the most severe ramp events are concentrated in Winter (DJF) and Autumn (SON), with Spring (MAM) events also represented. This confirms that the highest ramp stress aligns with periods of lower solar output and higher heating-driven demand recovery in the evening.

### 5 — Wind and Solar Perform at Measurably Different Rates
Embedded wind achieves an average capacity factor of **29.53%** — available continuously regardless of time of day. Embedded solar averages **10.24%** across the full year — reflecting zero output at night and strong seasonal variation. These two assets behave very differently and cannot be treated as equivalent flexibility contributors.

### 6 — GB Is a Net Electricity Importer on Average
The net interconnector flow KPI of **+3,330 MW** (positive = net import) confirms that GB consistently imports more electricity than it exports on average across the periods in this dataset.

### 7 — Pump Storage Consumes 172.48 MW on Average
Pumped storage is an active participant in the system across the dataset, consuming **172.48 MW on average** across all half-hourly periods. The intra-day distribution of this activity — when storage is charging versus discharging — is shown in the dashboard charts.

### 8 — No Causal Claims Are Made
The dashboard explicitly states: *"This page shows observational co-movement only. No causal relationships are implied or claimed."* All flexibility-context findings are presented as observed patterns in the data, not as evidence of cause and effect.

## Skills Demonstrated

### Microsoft Power BI
- Five-page interactive dashboard with cross-filtering slicers, KPI cards, heatmaps, area charts, dual-axis line charts, bar charts, and ranked tables

### Power Query (M Language)
- DateTime column constructed from settlement period arithmetic (Period 1 = 00:00 through Period 48 = 23:30)
- Calendar dimension creation: Year, Month, Season, Day of Week, Weekend Flag, Peak Period Flag
- Calculated columns: Gross Underlying Demand, Suppression Volume, Net Interconnector Flow (sum of 10 interconnector columns)
- Data quality checks: null validation, negative value checks, clock-change period handling

### DAX Measures
- `DIVIDE()` for suppression ratio and capacity factor calculations (avoids divide-by-zero)
- `AVERAGEX()` for half-hourly profile aggregations across settlement periods
- Window function logic (`OFFSET` / `EARLIER`) for half-hourly ramp rate calculation within each day
- `MAX()` / `MIN()` for peak and minimum demand identification
- Explicit measures table — no implicit Power BI aggregations used for KPIs

### Data Modelling
- Star schema: fact table + Date dimension + Settlement Period dimension
- Dedicated Measures table for all KPI calculations
- Correct cardinality and relationship management

### Energy Domain Knowledge
- GB settlement periods: 48 half-hourly periods per day, SETTLEMENT_DATE and SETTLEMENT_PERIOD structure
- National Demand (ND) vs Transmission System Demand (TSD) vs gross underlying demand
- Embedded generation concepts: distribution-connected assets, capacity factors, suppression effect
- Duck curve dynamics: midday solar suppression, evening ramp, operational significance
- Interconnector and pumped storage roles in GB system balancing

### Analytical Judgement
- Observational framing maintained throughout — co-movement presented without causal claims
- Data limitations acknowledged explicitly within the dashboard
- KPI selection driven by analytical meaning, not surface-level aggregates
