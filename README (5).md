# Project Title

**Behind the Meter: GB Embedded Renewable Impact, Duck Curve Dynamics & System Flexibility Pressures**

# Summary

This Power BI project analyses how embedded wind and solar generation relate to electricity demand, demand suppression, renewable capacity factors, demand ramping, interconnector flows, and pumped-storage activity in Great Britain.

The dashboard uses quantified KPIs and time-based comparisons to show the scale of embedded renewable contribution and the flexibility pressures visible in the analysed electricity-system data.

# Business Problem

Embedded renewable generation can reduce the electricity demand visible to the system compared with gross underlying demand. This creates a need to measure the difference, understand how the difference changes by time and season, and examine how quickly National Demand can rise.

The project also reviews interconnector flows and pumped-storage activity alongside positive demand ramps. These comparisons are observational only. The dashboard does not claim that interconnector behaviour or pumped-storage activity causes ramp events.

# Business Impact

- **Average gross underlying demand is 30.24K MW**, showing the average scale of electricity demand before the effect of embedded generation is reflected in National Demand.
- **Peak National Demand is 46K MW**, demonstrating the highest visible system-demand level reported by the dashboard.
- **Average suppression volume is 4.08K MW**, with a **13.50% suppression ratio**. This quantifies the average difference associated with embedded renewable generation relative to gross underlying demand.
- **Maximum suppression volume reaches 17K MW**, showing that the difference between gross underlying demand and visible demand can become substantially larger during some periods.
- **Average evening ramp rate is 879.10 MW**, while the **maximum ramp rate is 3,693 MW per 30 minutes**. These figures measure the scale and speed of increases in National Demand that system planners and operators need to observe.
- **Average net interconnector flow is 3.33K MW**. The dashboard uses this measure to provide context alongside positive demand ramps, without claiming a causal relationship.

# Objectives

- Compare National Demand with gross underlying demand by settlement period.
- Measure renewable-related demand suppression by volume and percentage.
- Examine embedded solar and wind generation by settlement period and month.
- Compare solar and wind capacity factors across time periods.
- Measure positive National Demand ramping, including evening and maximum ramp rates.
- Identify seasonal and monthly patterns shown in the dashboard.
- Review interconnector flows and pumped-storage activity alongside demand ramps without making unsupported causal claims.

# Key Findings

- The dashboard reports **4.08K MW average suppression volume** and a **13.50% suppression ratio**, quantifying the average renewable-related difference between gross underlying demand and visible National Demand.
- Suppression volume reaches a maximum of **17K MW**, showing that the measured difference varies significantly across the analysed periods.
- Renewable contribution is reported as **13.50%**.
- Peak wind capacity factor is **89.65%**, compared with a peak solar capacity factor of **63.48%**.
- The **average evening ramp rate is 879.10 MW**, while the **maximum ramp rate reaches 3,693 MW per 30 minutes**, showing that some increases in National Demand are much larger than the average evening increase.
- **January** is identified as the peak National Demand month.
- The three highest positive ramp-rate values displayed in the settlement-period chart are **1,870 MW**, **1,643 MW**, and **1,381 MW**.
- Average net interconnector flow is **3.33K MW**, and average pumped-storage pumping is **172.48**. The dashboard presents their relationship with ramp events as observational and does not establish causation.
